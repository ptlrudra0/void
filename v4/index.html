void

Phishing triage for links and emails.
Scan a link

Paste a link.
Get a verdict.

Six machine-learning models read the link itself and vote, in your browser. Three extra checks run on top: blocklists, look-alike brands and domain age. Only the domain name (never the full link) is sent out, to the public RDAP registry, for the age check.

How it works: paste a link. Six models score it from its text only (length, dots, symbols, words). The page is never opened. Verdict = their average; 50%+ means phishing. Trusted domain = on our known-safe list (e.g. charusat.ac.in), so not flagged.

Triage a suspicious
email or a list of links.

The workflow a security team follows: paste it, get a risk tier and reasons for every link, then export it for your ticket. The analysis runs in your browser. Nothing you paste is sent anywhere, and no link is ever opened.

1. Paste a full email (with headers) or a list of links. 2. Void pulls out every link and checks the sender. 3. Each link gets a tier (High, Medium, Low) with reasons. 4. Download CSV or JSON, or copy an incident summary.

Not measured: the email sender and wording checks (Reply-To mismatch, display-name brand, failed SPF/DKIM, pressure words, link text vs destination) are plain rules. Install and offline

Add to Home Screen from the browser share menu to open Void like an app. After the first visit it can reopen offline for the model checks. Blocklist, OpenPhish and domain-age lookups still need internet. Not tested on an iPhone yet.

Each one is measured below. None of them changes the six models or their numbers above.

1. Look-alike brands (rules, works offline)

Flags hosts that contain a brand name but are not that brand's real domain (paypal-secure-login.xyz, paypal.com.evil.xyz), typos (paypall.com), swapped characters (paypa1.com) and punycode. Checks 45 brands.

Test on 1,000 made-up look-alike links (20 brands x 5 patterns x 10, generated by us, so this is a synthetic test). Share caught as phishing:

On real links the gain is small. Only 0.5% of 3,000 held-out PhishTank links and 4.3% of 300 current OpenPhish links imitate a brand in the hostname; the rule caught 2 and 1 extra links the models had missed. Cost: it also flags 0.45% of the top 100,000 sites (for example adobe.net) and 0.31% of our 14,736 legitimate links, which is why a rule-only hit shows as "suspicious", not "phishing".

2. Blocklists (lookup, not prediction)

The live OpenPhish feed is fetched when you open the page. A saved snapshot of 392,090 known phishing domains (Phishing.Database, 16 Oct 2026) is checked too.

The snapshot found the host of 492 of 3,000 held-out PhishTank links (16.4%) and 10 of 300 OpenPhish links (3.3%). It wrongly matched 13 of the top 100,000 sites (0.013%) and 4 of 14,736 crawled legitimate links. Lists already feed on PhishTank and OpenPhish, so these hit rates say how much is already reported, not how well it predicts new sites. A list only knows what was reported before.

3. Domain age (RDAP registry lookup)

Looks up when the domain was registered. Shown as information, it does not change the verdict. Sites on shared platforms (vercel.app, weebly.com, blogspot.com...) and raw IP addresses are skipped, because the platform's age says nothing about the page.

What is not in v3, and why

  • Page content checks (login forms, hidden forms, logos). The page has to be downloaded first. Browsers block that across sites, and a proxy would send your links to a third party.
  • Google Safe Browsing, URLhaus, PhishTank live API. They need a private key or block browser requests.
  • Retraining. The six models are unchanged, so every number above the new section is the same as v2.

Read this.

  • URL only. 29 features from the text of the link: length, dots, hyphens, digits, entropy, @, IP, https, suspicious words. The page itself is never opened.
  • Six models. Logistic Regression, Decision Tree, Random Forest, linear SVM, XGBoost and a small neural net, trained with scikit-learn and XGBoost, exported to JavaScript.
  • Trusted-domain rule layer on top of the models: exact matches on well-known domains and CHARUSAT sites (charusat.ac.in, charusat.edu.in incl. e-Governance, charusat.online) are not flagged. Lookalikes such as charusat.ac.in.evil.com are not trusted and go through the models. Shared hosts like sites.google.com or github.io are never trusted by this rule.
  • Limits. Legitimate links came from popular site homepages, so real-world accuracy can be lower. Some safe links get flagged. Treat this as a student prototype, not a security product.
  • Verdict is the average of the six models, cut-off 50%.
